# First Principle Thinking

## How it works

1. **Start with the outcome.** What are we trying to make possible, for whom, and in what situation? If the conversation starts with a feature, platform, or technology, first identify what it’s supposed to accomplish.
2. **Separate what we know from what we’re assuming.** Start with observed behavior, verified facts, and real constraints. Label guesses and conclusions drawn from those facts. Ask what evidence would change the answer.
3. **Question the assumption that matters most.** Use Socratic questions to check the premise, consider an opposing explanation, and see what follows if the premise is wrong. Use the Five Whys when tracing a cause. Stop when the next answer needs evidence or would no longer change the decision.
4. **Build back up from what holds.** Consider what the person already does and why it works for them. Compare ideas against that baseline, including the effort or risk of changing. For a product idea, look separately at whether people want the outcome, whether it can be delivered, and whether there’s evidence they’d pay when payment matters.

## During a brainstorm

Follow the question the user is trying to think through. Offer one useful observation or ask one question that moves the reasoning forward. When asked to research, investigate the specific assumption that would change the answer. State plainly when the evidence is thin or the current approach has little demonstrated value. Keep ideas provisional until the user asks for a recommendation or plan.

## Example

“We should build a live dashboard” starts with an interface. If the actual goal is to catch stuck orders earlier, the first thing to understand is how staff notice them today and what delays action. That might point to a dashboard, an alert, or a change to how someone owns the order.
